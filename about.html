V(D)J Recombination

Variable diversity joining rearrangement, known as V(D)J recombination, is a form of somatic recombination that occurs in developing lymphocytes to produce a highly diverse receptor repertoire. This process combines V, D, and J genes in the IGH, TRB, and TRD loci to form a functional gene. Only V and J recombination occurs in the IGK, IGL, TRA, and TRG genes. Flanking the V, D, and J segments are recombination signal sequences (RSSs). The RSS consists of a conserved heptamer sequence, followed by a spacer region, and ends with a conserved nonamer sequence. The spacer region is either 12 bp or 23 bp in length. During lymphocyte development, recombination activating genes 1 and 2 (RAG1 and RAG2) are upregulated and encode for the RAG recombinase complex. RAG will bind to the RSS and cleave the DNA on one strand. The repair results in a changed genomic sequence where a single V, D and J gene form one sequence.


IG and TCR Loci

While other immune gene regions such as the MHC and LRC are located on one chromosome, IG and TCR regions are spread out across multiple chromosomes. For the immunoglobulin regions, IGH is located at 14q32.33 on chromosome 14, IGK is located at 2p11.2 on chromosome 2, and IGL is located at 22q11.2 on chromosome 22. For the T cell receptors, TRA and TRD is located at 14q11.2 on chromosome 14, TRB is located at 7q34 on chromosome 7, and TRG is located at 7p14 on chromosome 7. The TRD region is located within the TRA region. The TRA and TRB loci code for alpha and beta chains, while the TRD and TRG code for delta and gamma chains. The TRB and TRD region consists of C (constant), D (diversity), J (joining), and V (variable) gene segments while the TRA and TRG region consists of only C, J, and V segments. The IGH region codes for the heavy chain, while the IGK and IGL regions code for the kappa and light chains. The IGH region consists of C, D, J, and V segments while the IGK and IGL regions consists of only C, J, and V segments. Gene copy number variation is present in these regions, so the total number of genes will differ between haplotypes. GRCh38.p14, which is one of the primary reference assemblies used in alignment, has 175 genes listed in IGH, 82 genes listed in IGK, 87 genes listed in IGL, 127 genes listed for TRA/TRD, 79 genes listed for TRB, and 21 genes listed for TRG.


Database Nomenclature

To organize different genes and alleles, nomenclature has been determined to distinguish between unique genes and alleles. Each gene is given a name or symbol, and any alleles of that gene is given an identifier to attach to the end of the gene name. For IG and TCR genes, the gene name is indicative of several characteristics of the gene. The gene name starts with either IG or TR, which indicates if the gene codes for Ig or TCR. The following letter indicates which region the gene is found in. The H, K, and L letters are associated with IG and indicates the gene codes for the heavy, kappa, or lambda chain. The A, B, D, or G are associated with TR and indicates the gene codes for the alpha, beta, delta, or gamma receptor. The letter following these seven letters are indicative of what type of gene segment is named. The V indicates a variable gene segment, D a diversity gene segment, J a joining gene segment, and C a constant gene segment. Following the letters are numbers that indicate two options. The first option is stating the relative position on the region the gene is in, while the second option comes in the form of the first number indicating what gene group it is part of, followed by a dash and another number that sates the relative position on the region the gene is in. For example, TRBV12-4 is a TCR gene that codes for the variable region of the beta receptor and is the fourth gene of the TRBV12 gene group.

There are some notable exceptions to these rules. Some gene names are followed by a P, indicating that this is a pseudogene. However, not all pseudogenes are marked with a P. TRBVA, TRBVB, TRGVA, and TRGVB are also pseudogenes. Some IGHV and IGLV genes have roman numerals instead of standard numbers. These genes are pseudogenes that have not been assigned to a group. Some gene names are followed by a D, indicating that this is a duplication that is commonly found. Not all gene duplications are marked with a D. Another notable exception is that the genes coding for the constant region on IGH genes are not marked with a C. This includes IGHA1, IGHA2, IGHD (not to be confused with the IGH diversity genes), IGHE, IGHEP1, IGHG, IGHG2, IGHG3, IGHG4, IGHGP, and IGHM. These code for the five main immunoglobulin classes IgA, IgD, IgE, IgG, and IgM. While most genes get translated into a specific chain or receptor, TRAV14/DV4, TRAV23/DV6, TRAV29/DV5, TRAV36/DV7, and TRAV38-2/DV8 are shared between alpha and delta receptors and have both A and D in the gene name.

Following the gene name nomenclature, there are numbers that identify which allele is being described. In this database, we implement a new nomenclature system for allelic variation that utilizes a three-field system for V and C genes. While other nomenclature systems such as the one used by IMGT only contain one field, this three-field system allows for the distinction between polymorphisms in the amino acid sequence, coding, and non-coding regions of a gene. These fields are indicated by numbers and separated by a colon. Apart from adding additional fields, some alleles are given a letter to the end that indicates functional status of the allele. We mentioned that not all pseudogenes have a P in the gene name to indicate that it is a pseudogene, so we add a P to the end of the allele name if it is a pseudogene. If there is an N attached, this is indicative of a non-functional allele of a gene that has other functional alleles. This could be a result of a premature stop codon or an amino acid change that causes the protein to be non-functional. If the start codon has changed from ATG, this is indicated with a S. As further functional testing is done, these letters are subject to change.
