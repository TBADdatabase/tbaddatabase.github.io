TCR and BCR Loci

The TCR loci is comprised of four distinct loci (TRA, TRB, TRD, and TRG), while the BCR loci is comprised of three distinct loci (IGH, IGK, and IGL).The TRA (chr 14) and TRB (chr 7) loci code for alpha and beta chains, while the TRD (chr 14) and TRG (chr 7) code for delta and gamma chains. The TRB and TRD loci consists of C (constant), D (diversity), J (joining), and V (variable) segments while the TRA and TRG loci consists of only C, J, and V segments. The IGH locus codes for the heavy chain, while the IGK and IGL loci codes for the kappa and light chains.

V(D)J Recombination

Variable diversity joining rearrangement, known as V(D)J recombination, is a form of somatic recombination that occurs in developing lymphocytes to produce a highly diverse receptor repertoire. Flanking the V, D, and J segments are recombination signal sequences (RSSs). The RSS consists of a conserved heptamer sequence, followed by a spacer region, and ends with a conserved nonamer sequence. The spacer region is either 12 bp or 23 bp in length. During lymphocyte development, recombination activating genes 1 and 2 (RAG1 and RAG2) are upregulated and encode for the RAG recombinase complex. RAG will bind to the RSS and cleave the DNA on one strand. The repair results in a changed genomic sequence where a single V, D and J gene form one sequence.

Nomenclature

This database includes a new nomenclature system that includes a three field system for V and C genes. While other nomenclature systems such as the one used by IMGT only contain one field, this three field system allows for the distinction between polymorphisms in the amino acid sequence, coding, and non-coding regions of a gene.
